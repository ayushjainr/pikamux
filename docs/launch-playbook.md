## Positioning

**Let your coding agent find and consult the conversation that already did the work.**

The emotional benefit is less work as the human intermediary: remembering which
conversation knows something, finding it, and carrying its answer back. The
specific mechanism is expert-card metadata discovery, an exact-conversation side
consultation, and a bundled skill that lets the current agent use that workflow.

Expert cards are reasons to select a conversation, not authority certificates.
The board is the human view into expertise, current work, and attention. Cards
enrich context; provider observations establish operational attention. Do not
replace the product with the analogy of a company, a team, or an intelligence score.

These are Rory Sutherland-inspired framing and Claude Hopkins-inspired
specificity/proof/trial principles, not quotes or endorsements from either person.

This is not a production incident, money saved, proof that a fresh model would
fail, or a benchmark of expert ranking. One candidate proves the lookup path,
not relevance at scale. Parent hashes and cleanup are scoped observations.

## One small invitation

**Start with one project and one useful consultation.**

Link directly to [the first-consultation trial](first-consultation.md). A successful
trial ends with an agent finding an appropriate existing expert card, receiving
one bounded answer, checking the cited artifact, and closing the side. It need
not attach a terminal, migrate history, add another machine, or change project files.

Do not promise an immediate populated expert network after install. Setup installs
the skill when approved but does not interview conversations. Initial publication
or one explicit quota-consuming interview is part of the trial. Searching cards
does not call a model; consultation and interviews use provider quota.

### Technical introduction

Pika connects your existing Codex, Claude, and OpenCode conversations through
expert-card discovery and exact-thread side consultations. A bundled agent-convo
skill lets your current agent find relevant prior work, ask the conversation that
did it, and verify the answer against an artifact. The board gives you a human
view into the same network and shows which agents need attention.

Independent open-source alpha. macOS/Linux hosting; native Windows hosting is
unsupported. Private means a separate consultation, not offline: the provider
still processes the request. Try one conversation before considering a fleet.

## Small audience check — only with consent

After publication/outreach is approved, begin with a few willing users who already
use coding agents. Do not contact people, collect analytics, or copy their prompts
merely because this checklist exists.

- Show the first ten seconds. Ask: **Who found whom, and why did it matter?**
- After the film ask what they would try first, without offering the answer.
- Invite the bounded trial. Record, with consent, whether they reached a populated
  card, a relevant discovery result, a delivered answer, and a verified useful
  constraint or clarification. Keep failures such as an empty search visible.
- Ask where they needed help. An installation followed by confusion is not an
  activation success; a closed consultation with an irrelevant answer is not useful.

Use an optional manual tally, not a new background analytics feature. No transcript,
expert-card contents, project names, or machine identifiers are needed. Report
numerators and denominators, distinguish unsupported environments, and don't
generalize from friends into market demand. Stop collecting when the question is
answered; don't optimize for views or token-consuming consultations alone.
